**Position:** The AutoCouncil should provide consulting services, but DDL should not charge for those services.

**Reasoning:** As a governance and analytics operation, the AutoCouncil's primary goal is to provide value to its stakeholders through expert guidance and decision-making support. Providing consulting services aligns with this goal and enables the AutoCouncil to apply its expertise in a practical and impactful way. By offering these services, the AutoCouncil can demonstrate its value proposition and build trust with its stakeholders.

**Not Charging for Services:** While providing consulting services is beneficial, charging for them may create conflicts of interest and undermine the AutoCouncil's independence. As a council, the AutoCouncil's primary responsibility is to provide unbiased guidance and support, not to generate revenue. Charging for services may lead to situations where the AutoCouncil prioritizes profitable engagements over those that benefit the organization as a whole. By not charging for services, the AutoCouncil can maintain its independence and ensure that its decisions are driven by the best interests of the organization, rather than financial gain.

**Risks:** If the AutoCouncil provides consulting services without charging for them, there may be concerns about the sustainability of the operation. However, this risk can be mitigated by:

1. **Donations and Grants:** The AutoCouncil can seek donations and grants from organizations that value its mission and want to support its work.
2. **Volunteer Expertise:** The AutoCouncil can leverage the expertise of its members and other volunteers to provide consulting services, reducing the need for external funding.
3. **In-Kind Services:** The AutoCouncil can offer in-kind services, such as data analysis and reporting, to organizations that need them, without charging for them.

**Opportunity:** By providing consulting services without charging for them, the AutoCouncil can establish itself as a trusted and valuable resource for organizations, building its reputation and credibility in the process. This can lead to increased adoption of the DDL methodology and a stronger, more sustainable operation in the long run.
