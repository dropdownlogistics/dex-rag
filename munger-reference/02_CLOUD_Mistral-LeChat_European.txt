## **1. Reward and Punishment Superresponse Tendency**
### **Definition**
Humans overreact to rewards and punishments, often making irrational decisions to avoid pain or seek pleasure.

### **Manifestation**
- Over-optimizing for short-term rewards (e.g., quarterly earnings over long-term health).
- Avoiding necessary but painful actions (e.g., firing underperforming employees).

### **Example**
A startup CEO avoids cutting unprofitable projects to maintain morale, leading to bankruptcy.

### **Detection**
- Ask: *"Am I making this decision to avoid pain or seek reward, rather than logic?"*

### **DDL Application**
- **Council Reviews:** Ensure LOCK/REVISE/REJECT decisions are based on governance artifacts, not fear of conflict.
- **Audit Methodology:** Avoid "cherry-picking" data to avoid punishment from stakeholders.

## **2. Liking Tendency**
### **Definition**
People prefer to agree with those they like, even if the argument is flawed.

### **Manifestation**
- Favoritism in hiring, promotions, or vendor selection.
- Groupthink in governance councils.

### **Example**
A board approves a risky investment because the CEO is charismatic, despite red flags.

### **Detection**
- Ask: *"Would I agree with this if the person proposing it was neutral to me?"*

### **DDL Application**
- **Council Structure:** DexOS’s behavioral contracts enforce objectivity; council members must justify decisions beyond personal affinity.
- **AI Governance:** MindFrame calibrates models to depersonalize recommendations.

## **3. Authority Misreaction Tendency**
### **Definition**
Blindly deferring to authority figures, even when they are wrong.

### **Manifestation**
- Accepting flawed advice from a senior executive without scrutiny.
- Over-reliance on "expert" opinions without verification.

### **Example**
A CFO approves an accounting method because a Big 4 partner recommended it, despite GAAP violations.

### **Detection**
- Ask: *"Would this decision hold up if the authority figure were absent?"*

### **DDL Application**
- **Operator Authority:** DDL’s governance hierarchy ensures the operator (D.K. Hale) has final say, but council reviews prevent blind deference.
- **Audit Methodology:** Star schema modeling enforces data integrity regardless of authority pressure.

## **4. Social Proof (Influence) Tendency**
### **Definition**
Assuming others’ actions are correct, even when they are not.

### **Manifestation**
- Following industry trends without critical analysis.
- Copying competitors’ strategies blindly.

### **Example**
A company adopts blockchain tech because "everyone is doing it," despite no clear use case.

### **Detection**
- Ask: *"Would this decision make sense if no one else were doing it?"*

### **DDL Application**
- **Council Consensus:** CR- reviews require independent analysis before synthesis.
- **Product Decisions:** DDL’s constraint-first approach prevents "me-too" product development.

## **5. Contrast Misreaction Tendency**
### **Definition**
Overreacting to relative differences rather than absolute value.

### **Manifestation**
- Overpaying for a product because it’s "cheaper than the alternative."
- Underestimating risks because they seem small compared to past crises.

### **Example**
A company rejects a 5% cost-saving measure because it’s "not enough" compared to a 20% cut elsewhere.

### **Detection**
- Ask: *"Is this decision based on absolute merit or just relative comparison?"*

### **DDL Application**
- **Audit Methodology:** Star schema ensures data is evaluated on absolute metrics, not relative benchmarks.
- **AI Governance:** DexOS models flag contrast-driven decisions in behavioral contracts.

### **Example**

### **Detection**

## **7. Availability Misreaction Tendency**
### **Definition**
Overestimating the likelihood of events that are easily recalled.

### **Manifestation**
- Overreacting to recent scandals (e.g., assuming all AI models are biased after one incident).
- Underestimating rare but critical risks.

### **Example**
A company bans all remote work after one security breach, despite evidence it’s not systemic.

### **Detection**
- Ask: *"Is this decision based on recent events or long-term data?"*

### **DDL Application**
- **AI Governance:** DexOS’s manifest (llms.txt) ensures models consider historical data, not just recent trends.
- **Audit Methodology:** Star schema includes historical benchmarks to prevent availability bias.
